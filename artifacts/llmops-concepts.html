Developer Roadmap

LLMOps: Build, Prove, Ship, Operate

Your model is not the hard part. Everything around it is. Twelve practices that decide whether an LLM feature survives contact with real traffic, and the question each one answers.

Build→ Prove→ Ship→ Operate→ Repeat

Build

Make the inputs reproducible before you make anything else

01Datasets

Curate, version, hold out. Score on data you never trained on, and keep the holdout genuinely sealed.

How do you stop leakage?
02Prompt Versions

A prompt is code, not config. If you cannot diff it, you cannot debug it, and you cannot say what changed.

Who approved that prompt?
03Model Pinning

Pin the version, never ship against "latest". Providers deprecate on their schedule, not yours.

What happens when it is deprecated?

Prove

Turn opinion into a number that can block a release

04Eval Sets

Golden cases you must pass, with expected behaviour written down rather than remembered.

How big is your eval set?
05LLM as Judge

Score at scale, then audit the scorer against human labels. An uncalibrated judge is just a vibe with a number attached.

Do you trust the judge?
06Regression Gate

A regression blocks the ship. No green run, no release, and the eval suite owns that decision rather than a person.

What score blocks a ship?

Ship

Roll it out in a way you can undo

07Canary

A small slice of traffic, watched hard. Pick the abort metric before you start, not while it is burning.

What metric aborts it?
08Shadow

Run the new version silently on real traffic and compare after. The shadow answer never reaches a user.

Who pays for the shadow calls?
09Rollback

Undo in seconds, not hours. Rollback is a button you have already tested, not a plan you will write later.

How fast can you actually revert?

Operate

See what production is doing, and feed it back

10Tracing

One request, end to end, under a single id. Logs alone will not find the slow hop in a multi-step chain.

Can you replay one call?
11Cost Tracking

Tokens are the invoice. Measure price per resolved outcome rather than per call, since agents burn far more than chat.

What is your cost per resolved ticket?
12Feedback Loop

Today's bad output is tomorrow's eval case. A thumbs-down that goes nowhere is a defect you have agreed to keep.

Where do thumbs-downs go?
Twelve feeds one, and the loop closes.
Every production failure captured at step 12 becomes a golden case at step 1.
That single link is what turns a pile of practices into a system that gets better instead of just busier.
Roadmap: build reproducible inputs, prove quality with gates, ship behind canary and shadow with rollback ready, then trace, cost and feed failures back.
