Developer Roadmap

Kafka in 12 Concepts

Nobody fails a Kafka interview on the definition of a topic. They fail on ordering, on what a consumer group actually guarantees, and on what happens when a consumer dies mid-batch. These twelve, in the order they make sense.

Producer→ Topic→ Partition→ Broker→ Consumer

The write path

Getting events in, and deciding how they are laid out

01Producer

Sends events continuously into topics. Apps, databases and devices all publish the same way.

The key you choose decides the partition, and therefore the ordering.
02Topic

A named stream that groups related events. The unit you subscribe to.

A log you append to, not a queue you drain. Reading does not remove anything.
03Partition

Splits a topic so it can be processed in parallel while staying ordered inside each split.

Order is guaranteed per partition, never across a topic. This is the one people miss.

The read path

Getting events out, and remembering how far you got

04Consumer

Reads and processes events from topics at its own pace.

A slow consumer does not slow the producer. It just falls further behind.
05Consumer Group

Several consumers sharing the work, with partitions divided between them.

More consumers than partitions means the extras sit idle. Partitions cap your parallelism.
06Offset

Tracks each message's position in a partition, so a consumer knows where to resume.

When you commit the offset decides whether a crash replays work or loses it.

The cluster

Where the data physically lives, and how it survives a failure

07Broker

A server that stores partitions and serves reads and writes for them.

Each partition has one leader broker. Writes go there, not to any broker.
08Cluster

Many brokers together, giving scale and somewhere to fail over to.

Adding brokers does not rebalance existing partitions on its own.
09Replication

Copies each partition across brokers so a machine loss is not a data loss.

Acknowledgement settings decide whether "sent" means written to one replica or several.

Operating it

The parts that decide whether this stays workable at month six

10Retention

Messages are kept for a time or size limit, then dropped regardless of who read them.

A consumer down longer than the retention window loses those events permanently.
11Schema Registry

Manages the event formats passing between apps, and what counts as a compatible change.

Producers and consumers deploy separately, so schemas must stay compatible in both directions.
12Kafka Connect

Moves data between Kafka and outside systems without you writing the plumbing.

Most "we need a custom pipeline" cases are a connector plus configuration.
The partition is the whole idea.
Ordering, parallelism, consumer-group sizing and rebalancing all fall out of how a topic is partitioned.
Get the partition key right and most of the rest follows. Get it wrong and no amount of tuning downstream will save you.
Roadmap: publish into partitioned topics, consume in groups while tracking offsets, replicate across a cluster, then manage retention, schemas and connectors.
Pairs with System Design Map, where this underpins the messaging, feed and event-driven designs.
