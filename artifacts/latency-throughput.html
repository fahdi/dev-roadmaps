Latency, Throughput, Bandwidth, Jitter

Four different measurements that get called "speed". Move the sliders: widening the pipe does nothing to the round trip, and shortening the round trip does nothing to capacity. They are independent, which is why the wrong fix is so common.

Latency

Milliseconds

How long one round trip takes. Distance and the speed of light set a floor you cannot buy your way under.

Adding bandwidth never reduces it. A wider road does not shorten the journey, which is why a fast connection can still feel sluggish on a chatty protocol.

Throughput

Requests or bytes per second

What you actually get, as opposed to what the link could theoretically carry.

Bounded by whichever is worse: the link, the server, or the round trips your protocol insists on. It is a result, not a setting.

Bandwidth

Bits per second

The maximum the link can carry. The number sold to you, and a ceiling rather than a promise.

Capacity is not delivery. One connection with small windows and long round trips leaves most of a fat pipe idle.

Jitter

Variation in latency

How much the delay wobbles between consecutive packets. Averages hide it completely.

Irrelevant to a file download, decisive for voice, video and games, where arriving late and arriving unevenly are different problems.

Upgrading the connection is the usual answer and the usual mistake.
If pages feel slow because of round trips, more bandwidth changes nothing. If transfers are slow because the pipe is full, lower latency changes nothing.
Measure which one is actually binding before paying to fix the other.
Roadmap: separate the four, find the binding constraint, then fix that one.
