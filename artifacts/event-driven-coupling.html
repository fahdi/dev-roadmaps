Coupling: Direct Calls vs an Event Bus

Crash a service and watch what the rest of the system does about it. The usual telling stops at "the bus prevents the cascade", which is true and is only half of it: the work still did not happen, and now nobody is waiting to find out.

Direct calls

Synchronous, tightly coupled

Each service calls the next and waits. The caller needs the callee to be up, to be fast, and to still have the same interface it had last week.

A crash propagates backwards. Everything upstream is stuck holding a connection, and the customer holds a spinner.

In exchange you get the thing that is genuinely hard to give up: when the call returns, the work is definitely done.

Event bus

Asynchronous, loosely coupled

One service publishes a fact. Anyone who cares subscribes, and the publisher neither knows nor waits.

A crash stays local. The event waits in the queue, the other consumers carry on, and nothing upstream is blocked.

The price is that "accepted" no longer means "done". You now need retries, a dead-letter queue, and something watching it, or the failure is simply invisible.

The bus does not fix the failure. It changes who is inconvenienced by it.
Direct calls fail loudly at the customer. An event bus fails quietly in a queue.
Loud failure costs you a bad minute. Quiet failure ships an unpaid order and finds out on Tuesday.
Roadmap: decide whether this call must be true before you answer the customer. If it must, coupling is the feature, not the bug.
