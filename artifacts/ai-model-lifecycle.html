AI Model Lifecycle

Training, fine-tuning, serving and inference get used interchangeably, and they are four different activities with different costs, owners and failure modes. Knowing which one you are actually talking about resolves most confused conversations.

1

Training

Once, by very few

Build a foundation model from scratch on an enormous corpus. This is where the model's general capability comes from, and almost nobody reading this will do it.

ChangesEvery weight, from random initialisation
CostsLarge clusters, weeks, budgets most teams never see
WhoFrontier labs and a handful of others
2

Fine-Tuning

Occasionally, when behaviour must change

Adapt an existing model to a domain, format or style using your own examples. You are teaching behaviour, not adding facts.

ChangesAll weights, or a small adapter beside them
CostsHours to days, and a dataset someone has to curate
Watch forFine-tuning on facts, which go stale immediately
3

Serving

Continuously, as infrastructure

Put the model somewhere it can be reached: hosted behind an API, or running on hardware you operate. Nothing about the model changes here.

ChangesNothing in the model, everything about access
DecidesLatency, throughput, availability, data residency
LeversBatching, caching, replicas, hardware
4

Inference

Every single request

A prompt goes in, tokens come out. This is the only stage your users ever experience directly, and the only one billed per use.

ChangesNothing persists, each call starts fresh
CostsPer token, forever, scaling with adoption
Watch forTreating a one-off training cost like a running one
Same model, four different activities.
Training and fine-tuning change the model. Serving and inference use it.
Most "should we train our own model?" conversations are really about fine-tuning, and most fine-tuning proposals turn out to be a retrieval problem instead.
Roadmap: someone trains, you might fine-tune, you serve it somewhere, and inference is what you pay for from then on.
