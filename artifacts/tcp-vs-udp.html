TCP vs UDP

With a clean link the two are indistinguishable. The entire difference is what happens to packet 7 when packet 4 goes missing, so drop some packets below and watch when the application actually gets its data.

Protocol
What the application receives
delivered on arrival arrived on time, held back for an earlier packet lost on the wire, retransmitted
Delivered to app— of 12 sent
Worst stall— longest a packet waited after arriving
Stream complete— when the last packet reached the app

TCP

Ordered, reliable, stateful

socket(AF_INET, SOCK_STREAM)

Guarantees you get every byte, in the order they were sent. To keep that promise it must hold back everything that arrives after a gap until the missing piece is retransmitted.

That queue is head-of-line blocking, and it is not a bug. It is the cost of the guarantee you asked for.

UDP

Unordered, best effort, stateless

socket(AF_INET, SOCK_DGRAM)

Hands over whatever arrived, whenever it arrived. A lost datagram is simply absent, and nothing waits for it.

You get the freshest data available and the job of coping with a hole moves up into your application, where it can be a dropped video frame nobody notices.

Nobody chooses UDP because it is faster. They choose it because late data is worse than missing data.
A file transfer with a hole in it is corrupt, so it waits. A video call that pauses two seconds to recover one lost frame is worse than a call that skips it.
Ask what your application should do about a gap, and the protocol picks itself.
Roadmap: same wire, same loss, opposite promises. Decide whether a gap should stop the stream or be skipped past.
