DevSecOps and GitOps, End to End

Code to production in four phases, with the security gates that can actually stop a release. Every tool here is open source. Press run and watch a commit travel, or fail a gate and watch it stop.

Do you need SonarQube?

Probably not. It wants its own server and database to do something Semgrep does in seconds with rules as code and no infrastructure to run. In CI, that difference is minutes per build and one less stateful service to operate.

Its real draw is the quality-gate dashboard and the historical trend line, which genuinely matters if your organisation reports on code quality over time. For catching bugs, native linters plus Semgrep are faster and sharper.

Keep it for the dashboard and the org chart. Drop it for speed.

Do you need Argo CD?

The tool is replaceable. The pattern is not, and for one reason that rarely gets said out loud: with pull-based GitOps your CI never holds cluster credentials. The cluster reaches out to Git instead. That removes the most dangerous secret in the whole pipeline from the system most exposed to untrusted code.

You also get continuous drift correction and Git as the audit log. Flux is the lighter alternative, a set of focused controllers with no bundled UI.

Not on Kubernetes, or one cluster and one environment? Deploying from CI is fine.

What the common stack leaves out

Secret scanningNothing checks whether a key was committed. Gitleaks costs seconds and catches the incident you cannot revoke by rebuilding.
Signing and provenanceScanning proves an image was clean once. Signing with Cosign proves the thing running is the thing you built.
SBOMWithout a component inventory from Syft, the next big CVE starts with a week of guessing whether you are affected.
Admission policySigning means nothing if the cluster runs unsigned images anyway. Kyverno verifies the signature at admission.
Progressive deliverySyncing to Git state is not a release strategy. Flagger shifts traffic gradually and rolls back on metrics.
Logs and tracesMetrics tell you something broke. Loki and Tempo tell you what happened, through OpenTelemetry.
A gate that cannot fail the build is decoration.
The measure of a DevSecOps pipeline is not how many scanners are in the diagram. It is what happens when one of them finds something.
Signing without admission policy, scanning without a blocking threshold, and dashboards nobody is paged from all look identical on an architecture slide and do nothing at three in the morning.
Roadmap: scan the source, build reproducibly, sign what you ship, hand off through Git, let the cluster pull, then verify at admission and watch it converge.
